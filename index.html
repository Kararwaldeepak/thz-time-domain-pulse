Reading the waveform

What information is contained in a time-domain THz pulse?

01

Arrival time

The pulse position on the time axis tells when the THz transient reaches the detector. A material or waveguide can shift this arrival time.

02

Electric-field amplitude

The vertical value is the instantaneous electric field. Larger amplitude generally indicates a stronger detected THz field.

03

Field polarity and phase

Positive and negative lobes represent opposite field directions. Their exact shape carries phase information that intensity-only detectors discard.

04

Reflections and echoes

Delayed smaller pulses can arise from interfaces, substrates, windows, or waveguide reflections. Their delay gives optical-path information.

Do not confuse them

Electric field E(t) versus intensity E²(t)

Electric field

Contains sign, phase, polarity, temporal oscillation, and coherent interference.

Intensity

Is always non-negative. It is useful for pulse width and energy localization, but it removes field direction.

THz time-domain spectroscopy

How the waveform is measured experimentally

A THz-TDS system changes the relative delay between the THz pulse and an ultrashort optical gate. Each delay position measures one sample of the electric field. Repeating the process reconstructs the full waveform.

1

Generate

A femtosecond laser excites a THz emitter and produces a broadband transient.

2

Propagate

The THz pulse travels through free space, a sample, or a waveguide.

3

Sample in time

An optical delay changes when the gate pulse meets the THz field at the detector.

4

Reconstruct

The measured samples form E(t), and a Fourier transform produces amplitude and phase versus frequency.

Time-to-frequency connection

A short transient contains a broad range of frequencies

E(ω) = ∫ E(t)e−iωt dt

The time trace and spectrum are two representations of the same coherent signal. Changes in pulse shape therefore appear as changes in spectral amplitude and phase.

Main takeaway

A THz time-domain waveform is a coherent movie of the electric field.

Its timing, positive and negative lobes, amplitude, ringing, echoes, and noise all have physical meaning. The spectrum is not a separate measurement—it is obtained from the same waveform through a Fourier transform.
